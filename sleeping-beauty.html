Probability and self-location

Halfer or thirder?

Core insight

The two answers are not rival calculations of the same quantity. They answer two different questions. One asks how often the coin is heads. The other asks how often a waking observer finds herself in a heads world.

Question

On Sunday, Sleeping Beauty is put to sleep and a fair coin is flipped. If it lands heads, she is woken once, on Monday. If it lands tails, she is woken twice, on Monday and again on Tuesday. After each waking she is given a drug that erases the memory of that waking, so every awakening feels identical and she can never tell which day it is or whether it has happened before.

Each time she wakes, she is asked a single question. What is your credence right now that the coin landed heads? The coin is fair, so the answer is obviously \( 1/2 \). But she is twice as likely to be woken in a tails world, so the answer is obviously \( 1/3 \). Both arguments feel airtight. Which is right?

Why both numbers appear

Unlike a broken paradox, there is no single algebraic slip to point to here. The disagreement is about what event we are assigning probability to, and the question as stated does not fully pin that down.

The halfer view (\( 1/2 \)). The coin is fair, and Beauty knew in advance that she would be woken no matter how it landed. Waking up is therefore guaranteed under both outcomes, so it carries no new information. Her credence should equal her Sunday prior.

\[ P(\text{Heads}) = \tfrac{1}{2}. \]

The thirder view (\( 1/3 \)). Count the awakenings across many repetitions of the experiment. Heads produces one awakening. Tails produces two. From the inside, all three are indistinguishable, so each is equally likely to be the one Beauty is now experiencing.

\[ P(\text{Heads} \mid \text{awake}) = \frac{1}{\underbrace{1}_{\text{H, Mon}} + \underbrace{2}_{\text{T, Mon, Tue}}} = \frac{1}{3}. \]

Both are correct answers to different questions. The halfer weights coin flips. Over many runs, half of them land heads. The thirder weights observer-moments. Over many runs, only one waking in three occurs in a heads world. Tails simply manufactures more copies of the experience, and the puzzle never says whether Beauty should reason about the coin or about the waking she is inside.

The hidden assumption, then, is the belief that "the probability the coin is heads" has one meaning independent of the observer. When your own number of observations depends on the outcome, it does not.
